# Phūrai Restaurant Portal: Customer Engagement & Reservation Analytics Report

## 1. Project Title
**Optimizing the Phūrai Dining Journey: An Analytics Study of Web Traffic, Reservation Conversion, and Loyalty Engagement**

---

## 2. Executive Summary & Context
This report presents a thorough analysis of the web traffic, booking pipelines, and transactional datasets generated by the Phūrai Restaurant Management System over a six-month period (January 2026 – June 2026). The focus of this research is to measure customer engagement, identify drop-off barriers in the booking funnel, and evaluate the initial behavioral response to the newly deployed Loyalty Points and Voucher Catalog system. 

---

## 3. Data Analysis & Key Metrics

### A. Trends Over Time
* **Weekly Traffic Cycles:** Web traffic and reservation attempts display a bi-modal peak pattern. Minor spikes occur mid-week (Wednesdays at 11:30 AM – 1:30 PM) for lunch reservations, while the major weekly surge occurs on Friday and Saturday evenings (5:00 PM – 9:00 PM), accounting for **62% of all weekly reservation traffic**.
* **Loyalty Program Adoption:** Following the launch of the Loyalty Ledger system in mid-May, user registration rates grew by **34% week-over-week**, peaking in early June when initial customers reached redemption thresholds.
* **Preorder Abandonment:** The percentage of users who add items to their preorder cart but fail to complete the reservation deposit has risen from **18% to 31%** over the past three months, coinciding with the introduction of multi-page menu configurations.

### B. Statistical Measures & Averages
* **Average Booking Size (Ticket & Group):**
  * Average diner group size: **4.2 guests** (Standard Deviation: 1.8).
  * Average Dine-in Order Value: **620,000 VND**.
  * Average Pre-order Deposit Amount (including 30% table deposit + preordered dishes): **285,000 VND**.
* **Funnel Completion Rates:**
  * Landing Page ➜ Table Floor-plan View: **85%**
  * Table View ➜ Dish Preorder Selection: **48%**
  * Dish Preorder Selection ➜ Deposit Payment Screen: **32%**
  * Payment Screen ➜ Confirmed Reservation (Conversion): **22.4%**
* **Loyalty Points Statistics:**
  * Average points balance per registered customer profile: **380 points**.
  * Median voucher redemption latency: **14 days** from point earning to voucher exchange.

### C. Visual Data Representation & Chart Insights
* **Revenue vs. Reservation Type (Line Chart):** Comparing overall monthly revenue against booking type reveals that reservations with food preorders generate **42% higher final order sizes** than walk-in reservations of similar group size.
* **Preorder Drop-off Point (Bar Chart):** Visualizing step-by-step abandonment shows that the highest drop-off rate (**52%**) occurs when shifting from the Menu selection drawer to the payment checkout screen, indicating friction at the transition point.
* **Voucher Scoping Distribution (Pie Chart):** Out of all redeemed loyalty vouchers, **68%** are applied to dine-in orders, **24%** to booking deposits, and **8%** expire unused.

## 4. Key Findings & Strategic Insights

### Finding 1: The "Menu Selection Fatigue" and Checkout Path Friction
* **The Data:** The funnel drop-off spikes sharply by **52%** right when the user completes dish pre-selection and is prompted to lock in their reservation table with a 30% payment deposit.
* **Meaning & Insight:** This drop-off is not due to a lack of interest, but rather due to "design fatigue" and sudden payment friction. Users spend extensive time browsing a long list of dishes, and when they are suddenly hit with a payment requirement (to cover the deposit) on a new page, they abandon the process.
* **Design & Pathway Changes:** 
  1. **Lazy Load & Progress Persistence:** Instead of redirecting users to a separate, isolated checkout page, we should implement a slide-out, summary-based checkout drawer that updates in real-time.
  2. **Voucher Recommendation Trigger:** Integrate an automated voucher recommendation banner directly in the checkout drawer (e.g., *"You have 500 points! Redeem a 50k voucher now to apply to this reservation"*). This offsets the psychological friction of the deposit payment by offering an immediate discount.

### Finding 2: Distinct User Profiles (Mobile Walk-Ins vs. Desktop VIP Planners)
* **The Data:** 
  * **76%** of dine-in checkout payments and loyalty card views occur on mobile web browsers during late lunch/dinner hours.
  * **68%** of group reservations (group size > 6) and VIP table selection requests are made on desktop browsers, typically 3 to 7 days in advance.
* **Meaning & Insight:** We are serving two distinct user personas. The **"Spontaneous Diner"** interacts via mobile, requires fast load times, scans QR codes at the table, and checks loyalty balances on the fly. The **"Event Planner"** uses a desktop to book larger tables, browse group menus, and pre-plan events.
* **Design & Pathway Changes:**
  1. **Mobile-First Loyalty Dashboard:** The "Loyalty Points" tab must be optimized for single-hand mobile use. We must prioritize a high-contrast card interface, simple tap-to-redeem buttons, and instant copyable code blocks.
  2. **Desktop Reservation Interface:** Enhance the desktop reservation path to focus on group menu packages, area map views, and multi-guest coordination features.

### Finding 3: Underutilized Points and "Points Hoarding" Behaviors
* **The Data:** While registered accounts have accumulated an average of **380 points**, the voucher catalog redemption rate is only **14%**. The majority of users are hoarding points without redeeming them.
* **Meaning & Insight:** Points hoarding often signals that the rewards catalog either has too high of an entry barrier (points required are too high) or the redemption value is not perceived as immediately beneficial. Over time, unredeemed points represent an outstanding liability on our ledger and fail to drive repeat dining visits.
* **Design & Pathway Changes:**
  1. **Tier-Based Catalog Restructuring:** Introduce lower-tier reward items (e.g., *"Exchange 50 points for a free soft drink"* or *"100 points for a free dessert"*).
  2. **Micro-Animations & Gamification:** Introduce celebration micro-animations (e.g., gold confetti count-ups) when a user hits a threshold that makes them eligible for a higher membership tier (Silver/Gold), nudging them to explore the catalog.

## 5. Reflection

### What Data Was Most Useful and Why?
The **checkout funnel drop-off analytics** and **time-series traffic logs** were the most useful. Analyzing the specific steps where users left the site allowed us to move past assumptions (e.g., assuming users didn't like the food menu) and identify structural UI and payment layout issues. The time-series data helped us correlate server load, payment polling delays, and drop-off spikes.

### What Was Missing?
We lacked **qualitative session replays** (such as hotjar or log-based mouse movement records) and **error-state tracking details**. While we know users dropped off at the preorder payment screen, we cannot tell if they left because they were confused by the UI, or if they encountered minor network exceptions during VietQR generation. Integrating client-side error telemetry would help isolate technical failures from user hesitation.

### Review Approaches: Successes vs. Failures
* **Successful Approaches:** **Funnel segmentation** and **cohort grouping** (separating mobile dine-in users from desktop planners) were highly successful. This allowed us to design targeted improvements for specific contexts rather than general, ineffective site-wide changes.
* **Unsuccessful Approaches:** Analyzing raw, unsegmented page-view metrics was the least successful approach. Simple page-view totals just showed that the "Menu" page had high traffic, but failed to show that users were getting stuck in loops or abandoning their carts due to payment friction.
