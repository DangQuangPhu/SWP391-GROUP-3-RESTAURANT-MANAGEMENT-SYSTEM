# Affinity Wall Analysis & Executive Report

## 1. Project Title
**Affinity Analysis: High-Level Findings on Spotify Mobile Podcast Management**

---

## Section 1: Mini-Project Description

Spotify is a leading global audio streaming service with over 500 million active users. While the platform was originally built for music, it has integrated podcasts as a core feature of its mobile application. Within the "Your Library" tab, users can follow podcast shows, save individual episodes, download audio files for offline listening, and view automatically compiled playlists such as "New Episodes" and "Your Episodes." As podcast catalogs grow, users are increasingly using Spotify as a daily podcast management application, which requires sophisticated library sorting, storage administration, and consumption status tracking.

This needs assessment project is designed to understand how users interact with these podcast management interfaces. Specifically, the project will look at how users organize their upcoming listening queues and manage their device storage space. This scope is appropriate for two hour-long usability interviews because it focuses on a specific, self-contained set of features within the larger Spotify app. During the sessions, the researcher can observe users interacting with their "Your Library" tab, watch them attempt to sort or filter their podcast episodes, and listen to them describe their mental models for organizing audio content.

## Section 3: Summary of Higher-Level Findings

### Finding 1: Visual Ambiguity Between "Saved" and "Offline Downloaded" States Triggers User Navigation Errors
The primary finding from our affinity analysis is that Spotify's user interface does not clearly separate the concept of "saving" an episode from "downloading" it. This visual overlap creates a fragmented mental model for users, who frequently mistake saved playlist markers for offline-ready files. In our interviews, participants struggled to determine whether an item was stored locally on their device or was being streamed live over cellular networks, leading to accidental mobile data usage and navigation failures.

**Detailed Qualitative Evidence:**
* **Observation Data:** During the session with participant **P1**, they were observed navigating back and forth between the "Your Episodes" filter tab and the "Downloaded" category button three times before locating the specific offline files. This loop indicates that the user was looking for a single consolidated list but was forced to navigate split tabs.
* **Direct Quotation:** This confusion was explicitly articulated by **P1**, who stated: *"I download episodes before flying, but then I can never find where the downloaded list is hidden, so I end up just streaming them on cellular data by accident."*
* **Factual Evidence:** This state confusion has measurable impacts on user storage. Factual notes show that **P1** had 8.4 Gigabytes of local storage occupied by downloaded files without actively knowing which episodes were saved vs. downloaded.

---

### Finding 2: The Absence of Bulk Management Tools Forces High-Friction "Housekeeping" Workflows
The second major finding is that the lack of bulk selection and deletion tools in the "Your Library" section creates an unsatisfying "housekeeping" chore. For active listeners who follow multiple weekly shows, keeping the library clean is a tedious process. Because the interface only allows single-item operations, users are forced to perform repetitive tap-and-confirm patterns to clear finished files.

**Detailed Qualitative Evidence:**
* **Observation Data:** Participant **P2** attempted to clean up their library by long-pressing an episode card. They expected a checkmark-based multi-select mode to open (a standard mobile interface pattern). Instead, the app only displayed a single-item options popup, forcing the user to cancel and delete episodes one-by-one.
* **Direct Quotation:** **P2** expressed significant frustration with this limitation: *"I wish there was a simple 'Select All' or checkmark system so I could clean up my download list. Right now, it takes me five minutes of tapping just to clear space on my phone."*
* **Factual Evidence:** The volume of weekly updates exacerbates this issue. **P2** follows a total of 18 different podcast shows, with 6 of those updating weekly. Without a bulk-clear tool, managing these weekly additions leads to cumulative friction.
